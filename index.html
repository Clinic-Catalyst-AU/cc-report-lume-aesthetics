Task One

Branding & Positioning

1. Keep the look. Own the story.

Black, white and gold, the script accent, "confidence looks good on you". Nothing about your look changes. What is missing is the sentence underneath it: a cosmetic nurse in Bundaberg with a special interest in getting ahead of ageing, who plans skin and cosmetic medicine together so it stays natural. That sentence goes on the website, the bio, every ad and the front window.

2. Concern first, machine second

Nobody in Bundaberg searches for Pro Cell or Superlux. She notices texture, the first lines, a sun spot, hair she is sick of, a tattoo she regrets. The ad talks about that, the page explains what a consultation looks at, and the treatment gets chosen in the room by a nurse. Your collections already do this well. We take it all the way through.

3. Two nurses, two reasons to book

Angel is a second door, not a Friday special. Her own page, her own booking link, her own series on the socials she already runs. Fridays fill because people book Angel, and when the second room opens, the pattern is already there for the dermal therapist.

4. Advertising rules, handled once

After twelve months of being restricted, the rules matter to you. Cosmetic medicine is advertised as a consultation, never by product name, never with a price in the ad. Skin can be advertised openly, with prices. "Has a special interest in", never "expert". Every post Angel writes and every ad we run goes through the compliance filter before it goes out, so it cannot creep back in.
